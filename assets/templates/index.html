It’s Getting Harder to Defend Taylor Swift… When Everyone’s a Serious Illiterate

While it would have been a pleasure for a 19-year-old to discuss his favorite artist’s newest album, I believe that the sheer scale of vicious commentary directed toward Taylor Swift since the release of The Life of a Showgirl has become so alarmingly unified yet still unsurprisingly unsubstantiated that it requires someone who really never cared about “fandom wars” to weigh in with opinions that, for better or worse, are destined to end up unacknowledged.

It’s safe to say that, since Swift’s last release, The Tortured Poets Department, taking her lyrics out of context and twisting their meanings has become common practice on the internet. And that’s not unusual at all: every form of communication has a risk of being misinterpreted, and that risk really becomes a certainty if you’re actively hoping to place yourself at an advantage, be it moral or credibility, in the communication. It’s even more helpful to misinterpret what others are saying if you’re arguing with someone who, likely, has never picked up a pen in their whole life. Logical fallacy? Who cares. My job here today is to spoil the fun for a group of people who are having the time of their lives because they just got their newest annual (or biannual) dose of encouragement or relatability from someone they look up to.

And they even have a weird, corny name: Swifties. It’s a rather well-known fact that we’re talking about one of the biggest fan bases in the world. And I hopped on this bus late in 2020, the year folklore came out. It fills me with disappointment to see that, for some reason, folklore has become the spotlight and a watershed of differing voices on the new album since its release. But we’ll get to that on a different occasion.

Most prominent among the unadulterated voices of lambasting is the allegation that Taylor Swift, a woman who has repeatedly expressed her support of the Democratic Party, is a Republican. A significant portion of me felt I should really stop typing and close this document after I typed out that sentence, but let me walk you through some possible reasons why people are “guessing” the answer to a question that has been answered blatantly multiple times.

Throughout the majority of 2023 and 2024, Swift went on a tour called The Eras Tour. Spanning 21 countries and 21 months, it was a highly labor-intensive project for the singer, yet she still managed to deliver Speak Now (Taylor’s Version), 1989 (Taylor’s Version), and The Tortured Poets Department during the tour. It shouldn’t be hard to come to a consensus that, during these months of switching between onstage performing and offstage songwriting, with a pinch of heartbreaks and new relationships, Swift, unlike other artists who are not involved in a smothering workload, really didn’t have the time for commentaries on geopolitical conflicts and elections.

Yet she broke the silence on the evening of September 10, just after the Presidential Debate between Kamala Harris and Donald Trump, to endorse, and I really, really need your eyes wide open for these next two words: Kamal Harris.

Spin the dial clockwise: after her endorsement of Harris, Swift became the preferred individual for the humiliation rituals of the Republican Party, most notably from Donald Trump’s post on Truth Social, where he posted, and I quote, verbatim:

“I HATE TAYLOR SWIFT!”

Spin the dial counterclockwise: before endorsing Harris, even in the previous election cycle, she tweeted multiple times, addressing the division Trump has created in the nation and how Swift calls on people to “vote [him] out in November.”

Going further, half of a whole documentary about Swift, Miss Americana, released in 2020, elaborates on her journey to finally breaking her silence on political matters, and it was not an easy trip for her: she referenced how the Dixie Chicks got cancelled for their controversial take on former President Bush’s decision to invade Iraq. She talked about how she kept her mouth shut. She argued with her parents about the words that were coming out of her mouth.

But all of that comes to nothing when compared to the lyrics of “Wi$h Li$t,” one of the songs on the singer’s newest release, where she sings, “I just want you / Have a couple kids, got the whole block looking like you”. Or when she expressed her happiness in taking the next step with her current partner, Travis Kelce. People also went on a scavenger hunt for other “clues” pointing to her aligning with Republican beliefs, like how, in the opening lines of the second verse of “I Hate It Here,” a song on The Tortured Poets Department, she sings about how she and her friends “used to play a game where / We would pick a decade / We wished we could live in instead of this.” Her pick was:

“I'd say the 1830s but without all the racists / And getting married off for the highest bid,” Swift sings.

And of course, “without all the racists”, the four words in ink, just vanished under some people’s eyes. Posts started recycling themselves from one release cycle to another, garnering hundreds of thousands of likes on Twitter. How did that happen?

Circling back, I think we have the answer written all over:

What logical fallacy? Who cares.

And so off they went, appealing to emotion becoming everyone’s best weapon, ad hominem on her looks becoming their sharpest tool, and red herring becoming their favorite friend.

It’s getting harder to defend Taylor Swift when people are seriously and selectively illiterate, just like it’s hard to defend your position when a toy chihuahua in some stranger’s purse licked your child’s ice cream - you can't even be mad.
